Buying a diamond for the first time is one of the most significant purchases of your life — a

What Is Diamond Clarity? The Basics Explained
Diamond clarity refers to the presence or absence of internal characteristics (called inclusions)
and surface characteristics (called blemishes) within a diamond. These tiny imperfections are
formed deep within the earth during the diamond's natural creation process — or, in the case of
lab-grown diamonds, during the controlled growth process.
In simple terms: the fewer the inclusions, the higher the clarity grade, and the more brilliant
and valuable the diamond.
Inclusions can appear as tiny crystals trapped inside the diamond, feather-like fractures, clouds
of microscopic particles, or needle-shaped formations. Most inclusions are completely invisible
to the naked eye and can only be seen under 10x magnification by a trained gemologist.

at, and choosing the diamond that gives you maximum brilliance within your
budget." — Solitario Diamond Specialists
Why Does Diamond Clarity Matter?
Clarity directly impacts the diamond's brilliance, and overall visual appeal. A diamond with fewer
inclusions allows more light to pass through and refract, creating that breathtaking sparkle we all
love. However, the impact of clarity on appearance varies significantly across grades — and
that's where smart buying decisions are made.

America (GIA) and is used universally — including for certified lab-grown diamonds. The scale

Visual Clarity Chart Explanation: What Each Grade Actually
Looks Like
Since most buyers cannot view diamonds under a microscope before purchasing online, here is
a detailed visual description of what each clarity grade looks like — both under magnification
and to the naked eye.
FL and IF — The Pinnacle of Purity
Imagine looking through a perfectly clean glass window on a clear day — that is what an FL or
IF diamond looks like, even under 10x magnification. There are no dark spots, no white clouds,
no lines. The diamond appears completely transparent and pure. These are extraordinarily rare,
accounting for less than 1% of all gem-quality diamonds, and are typically reserved for
investment pieces or ultra-luxury jewellery. To the naked eye, they are absolutely flawless.
VVS1 and VVS2 — The Connoisseur's Choice
At VVS grades, inclusions exist but are so minute that even trained gemologists with 10x
magnification need time to locate them. Think of a tiny pinpoint of light — smaller than the
period at the end of this sentence — in the corner of a large room. That is the scale of a VVS
inclusion. To the naked eye: completely indistinguishable from a Flawless diamond. These
grades are favoured by discerning buyers who want near-perfection without the rarity premium
of FL/IF.
VS1 and VS2 — The Sweet Spot for Luxury Buyers
VS clarity diamonds contain minor inclusions that require 10x magnification to see clearly.
Under a loupe, you might notice a tiny crystal, a feather, or a small cloud — but these are firmly
within the diamond's structure and do not affect durability. To the naked eye: eye-clean in
virtually all cases. VS1 and VS2 represent the ideal balance of quality and value, which is why
Solitario's curated collection emphasises diamonds in this range and above.
SI1 and SI2 — Budget-Conscious with Caution
SI clarity diamonds have inclusions that are clearly visible under 10x magnification and may be
visible to a keen naked eye, depending on where the inclusion sits within the stone. An SI1 with
an inclusion near the edge (masked by the setting) can appear eye-clean, while an SI2 with a
central dark crystal may be noticeable. Careful selection is essential at this grade.
I1, I2, and I3 — Where Brilliance Begins to Suffer
In I-grade diamonds, inclusions are visible without magnification and can noticeably impact the
diamond's ability to reflect light. I1 might still be acceptable for very tight budgets, but I2 and I3
diamonds display visible cloudiness, dark spots, or cracks that diminish the diamond's beauty
significantly. Solitario does not carry I-grade diamonds in its collection, as we believe every
piece should sparkle with the brilliance you deserve.
Diamond Clarity in Lab-Grown Diamonds: What Indian Buyers
Should Know
Lab-grown diamonds are graded using the exact same clarity scale as mined diamonds — FL to
I3. The GIA, IGI (International Gemological Institute), and other leading bodies certify lab-grown
diamonds with the same rigour and precision.
Here's the advantage: because lab-grown diamonds are created in controlled environments, it
is significantly easier to produce high-clarity stones compared to mined diamonds, where
geological chaos determines the outcome.

Common Myths About Diamond Clarity — Debunked
Myth 1: "You Always Need a Flawless Diamond"
Reality: FL and IF diamonds are extraordinarily rare and command premium prices, but to the
naked eye, they look identical to a VS1. For wearable jewellery, a VS1 or VS2 diamond offers
the same visual beauty at a fraction of the cost. Reserve FL/IF for investment collections, not
everyday rings.
Myth 2: "SI Diamonds Are Always Visible"
Reality: An SI1 diamond with a well-placed inclusion (near the girdle, hidden under a prong) can
appear completely eye-clean. The key is to examine the diamond's clarity plot in its certificate
and ask your jeweller about inclusion placement. Solitario's specialists help you make this
distinction clearly.
Myth 3: "Higher Clarity Always Means More Sparkle"
Reality: Sparkle (brilliance) is primarily determined by a diamond's cut quality, not its clarity. A
perfectly cut VS2 diamond will outshine a poorly cut VVS1 every single time. When balancing
your 4Cs budget, prioritise cut first, then clarity.
Myth 4: "Lab-Grown Diamonds Have Lower Clarity"
Reality: Lab-grown diamonds can and do achieve FL clarity grades. In fact, because the growth
process is controlled, certain types of inclusions common in mined diamonds (like irregular
crystal trapping) are less frequent in lab-grown stones. Solitario's selection proves this — our
diamonds shine because they are genuinely exceptional.
Myth 5: "A Diamond with Inclusions Is Defective"
Reality: Inclusions are natural — they are the diamond's fingerprint, its birthmark. Even the
finest gemologists appreciate that inclusions tell the story of a diamond's formation. What
matters is whether the inclusion impacts brilliance or durability, not its mere existence.
How to Choose the Right Clarity Grade for Your Jewellery
Your ideal clarity grade depends on three factors: the size of the diamond, the style of setting,
and your personal priorities.
For Solitaire Rings (1 Carat and Above)
Larger stones show inclusions more readily, so VS2 or better is recommended. A 1.5-carat VS1
solitaire from Solitario will display pure, uninterrupted brilliance that becomes a conversation
piece for generations.
For Cluster or Halo Settings
In halo designs, smaller diamonds surround the centre stone, and the intricate setting naturally
draws the eye. Here, SI1 in the side stones and VS2 in the centre stone creates a beautiful,
practical balance.
For Earrings and Pendants
Since earrings and pendants are often viewed at a greater distance, VS2 to SI1 can work
beautifully and allow more budget to be allocated to carat weight and cut quality.
For Investment Pieces
If you're purchasing diamonds as a store of value or heirloom gift, VVS or FL-certified lab-grown
diamonds from Solitario represent a smart, ethical, and enduring choice.
Pro Tip from Solitario: Always request the full grading certificate (GIA, IGI, or
equivalent) with your diamond purchase. The clarity plot on the certificate shows
exactly where inclusions are located — this is your window into the diamond's
character.
Frequently Asked Questions About Diamond Clarity
Q: What is the best diamond clarity for everyday jewellery?
For everyday jewellery like engagement rings and daily-wear pendants, VS1 or VS2 is widely
considered the ideal clarity grade. These diamonds are eye-clean (no inclusions visible to the
naked eye), durable, and offer exceptional brilliance without the rarity premium of FL or IF
grades.
Q: What does the diamond clarity chart show?
The diamond clarity chart shows the GIA-established grading scale for diamonds, ranging from
Flawless (FL) at the top to Included 3 (I3) at the bottom. It categorises diamonds based on the
size, number, nature, position, and relief of inclusions and blemishes when viewed under 10x
magnification.
Q: Is VS1 or VS2 clarity better?
VS1 has fewer and smaller inclusions than VS2, but both are considered eye-clean grades that
look identical to the naked eye. VS2 offers slightly better value for money while maintaining the
same visual quality, making it one of the most popular clarity choices for premium diamond
jewellery.
Q: Can I see inclusions in a VS2 diamond with my naked eye?
In the vast majority of cases, a VS2 diamond is completely eye-clean — meaning no inclusions
are visible without magnification. Occasionally, in larger stones (2 carats and above), a VS2
inclusion near the table may be faintly visible under ideal lighting conditions, but this is
uncommon.
Q: Do lab-grown diamonds have better clarity than mined diamonds?
Lab-grown diamonds are graded on the same clarity scale as mined diamonds and can achieve
any grade from FL to I3. Because they are grown in controlled conditions, certain types of
inclusions are less prevalent, and it is generally more feasible to produce high-clarity lab-grown
diamonds at competitive price points — which is a significant advantage for buyers seeking
premium quality.
Q: What clarity grade does Solitario offer in its diamond jewellery?
Solitario curates its collection with a focus on VS and above clarity grades for centre stones,
ensuring that every piece from our collection is eye-clean, brilliantly cut, and certified. We
believe premium jewellery deserves premium materials — and our customers deserve nothing

Q: Is a Flawless diamond worth the price for an engagement ring?
For most buyers, a Flawless diamond is not necessary for an engagement ring. Since FL and IF
diamonds look identical to VS grades to the naked eye, the additional cost of a Flawless
diamond is best justified if the diamond is being purchased as a significant investment or
collector's item. For wearable luxury, VS1 delivers the same visual impact at a far more
accessible price.
Conclusion: Clarity Is the Confidence Behind Every Diamond
Diamond clarity is more than a grading system — it is a window into a diamond's soul.
Understanding clarity empowers you to make confident, informed choices that align with your

Understanding Gold Purity: What Do the Karats Actually
Mean?
Gold in its pure form is too soft for everyday jewellery. It bends, scratches, and deforms under
normal wear. To create durable, wearable pieces, pure gold is alloyed — blended — with other
metals such as copper, silver, zinc, or palladium. The karat (K) system measures how much of
the resulting alloy is pure gold.

Durability, Wearability, and Long-Term Value
The Durability Debate
A common argument in favour of 14K gold is its superior hardness. This is technically
accurate: higher alloy content does make 14K gold harder and marginally more scratch-
resistant than 18K. However, this argument deserves context.
18K gold is not fragile. It is used in the world's most prestigious jewellery — from Cartier to
Bulgari — precisely because its durability is more than adequate for everyday luxury wear. The
hardness difference between 14K and 18K gold is measurable in a materials science lab, but
virtually imperceptible in real-world jewellery use.
What matters far more than hardness is the quality of the alloy composition and the
craftsmanship of the setting. A poorly made 14K piece will fail sooner than a masterfully crafted
18K piece every single time.
Skin Sensitivity and Hypoallergenic Properties
One of 18K gold's significant advantages for Indian buyers is its skin compatibility. India's warm,
humid climate means jewellery is worn against skin that perspires more than in cooler climates.
The higher alloy content in 14K gold — particularly nickel, used in white gold — can trigger
dermatitis and allergic reactions in sensitive skin.
18K gold's higher purity means less reactive metal content, making it significantly more
hypoallergenic. For pieces worn daily — rings, earrings, bangles — this distinction becomes
practically important.
Tarnish and Oxidisation Resistance
Gold itself does not tarnish. It is the alloy metals in the composition that can oxidise over time,
particularly copper and silver. Because 18K gold contains less alloy content by volume, it is
more resistant to surface oxidisation — preserving its lustre and colour for longer without
maintenance.
14K gold, with 41.7% alloy metals, is more susceptible to dulling over time, particularly in humid
environments or when exposed to chlorine (swimming pools) or household chemicals.
For Indian buyers who wear their jewellery daily — through seasons,
celebrations, and the full richness of a lived life — 18K gold's tarnish resistance
and skin compatibility make it the more practical as well as the more prestigious
choice.

Frequently Asked Questions: 14K vs 18K Gold
Q: Is 18K gold better than 14K gold for diamond jewellery?
Yes, for most luxury buyers. 18K gold (75% pure) offers a richer colour, greater skin
compatibility, and superior tarnish resistance compared to 14K gold (58.3% pure). While
14K gold is marginally harder, 18K gold is durable enough for everyday fine jewellery and is
the internationally recognised standard for luxury diamond settings. Its deeper, warmer tone
also enhances the visual impact of diamonds, particularly in yellow and rose gold variants.
Q: Which is better for sensitive skin — 14K or 18K gold?
18K gold is significantly better for sensitive skin. Its higher gold content (75%) means less
reactive alloy metals such as nickel and copper, which are common causes of skin irritation
and allergic dermatitis. For Indian buyers who wear jewellery daily in warm, humid
conditions, 18K gold's hypoallergenic properties are a practical advantage over 14K.
Q: Does 18K gold tarnish?
18K gold has excellent tarnish resistance due to its high gold content. While no gold alloy is
entirely immune to surface dullness over time — particularly when exposed to harsh
chemicals or chlorine — 18K gold's lower alloy content means it oxidises far more slowly
than 14K gold. Routine cleaning with mild soap and a soft cloth maintains its lustre
indefinitely.
Q: What is the difference between 14K and 18K gold in terms of colour?
The colour difference is visible and significant. 18K yellow gold appears richer, deeper, and
more saturated than 14K yellow gold, which has a lighter, slightly diluted tone due to its
higher alloy content. In rose gold, 18K produces a deeper, warmer blush. In white gold, 18K
requires less rhodium plating and maintains its white tone more consistently. For jewellery
intended to look and feel luxurious, 18K gold's colour richness is a meaningful advantage.
Q: What does 18K gold vermeil mean?
18K gold vermeil refers to sterling silver jewellery coated with a layer of 18K gold at a
minimum thickness of 2.5 microns. It is distinct from standard gold plating, which uses base
metals and thin gold layers that wear off quickly. Quality gold vermeil — like Solitario's
pieces, coated at 3+ microns — delivers the full visual richness of 18K gold at a more
accessible price, making it an intelligent choice for luxury jewellery buyers.
Q: Is 14K or 18K gold better for an engagement ring in India?
18K gold is the preferred choice for engagement rings in India and globally. Its higher purity
delivers richer colour, better skin compatibility, and the prestige associated with fine
jewellery. An engagement ring is a piece worn every day for a lifetime — 18K gold's
combination of beauty, durability, and hypoallergenic properties makes it ideal for this
purpose. Solitario's engagement rings are crafted exclusively in 18K gold settings.
Q: Is 18K gold more expensive than 14K gold?
Yes. 18K gold contains 75% pure gold versus 14K's 58.3%, making the raw material more
valuable. This is reflected in the price of finished jewellery. However, the premium is justified
by a meaningfully superior aesthetic — richer colour, better skin compatibility, and the

Q: Which gold karat is used by luxury jewellery brands globally?
The vast majority of the world's premier luxury jewellery houses — including Cartier, Van

diamond jewellery. The international hallmark for 18K gold is 750, indicating 750 parts per

What Are Lab-Grown Diamonds, Really?
Before we talk durability, let us settle one foundational truth: a lab-grown diamond is not a
diamond simulant. It is not cubic zirconia. It is not moissanite. It is not glass with a sparkle filter.
A lab-grown diamond is a real diamond. Chemically, physically, and optically — it is identical
to a mined diamond. Both are composed of pure carbon atoms arranged in a cubic crystal
lattice structure, which is precisely what gives diamonds their legendary hardness and light-
refracting brilliance.
The only difference is origin. Mined diamonds form over billions of years deep within the earth
under extreme heat and pressure. Lab-grown diamonds replicate those exact conditions in a
controlled environment — either through High Pressure High Temperature (HPHT) or Chemical

The Science of Durability: Where Do Lab-Grown Diamonds

Hardness: The Mohs Scale
Hardness is measured on the Mohs scale, a 1–10 system that ranks minerals by scratch
resistance. Diamond — whether mined or lab-grown — scores a perfect 10 out of 10. It is the
hardest naturally occurring substance on Earth.

Structural Integrity
Beyond hardness, a diamond's toughness — its resistance to breaking or chipping — is also
remarkable. Diamonds have a toughness rating of 7.5–8 MPa·m½, placing them among the
most structurally robust gemstones available. Normal daily wear — including cooking, typing,
and casual exercise — poses no meaningful threat to a well-set diamond.
Thermal and Chemical Stability
Lab-grown diamonds are chemically inert. They do not react to household chemicals, perfume,
soap, chlorine, or sweat. They are not affected by heat from sunlight or cooking environments.
The crystal structure that makes them so hard also makes them chemically stable across a wide
range of everyday conditions.
A lab-grown diamond's Mohs hardness score of 10 means that only another
diamond can scratch it. No daily activity, cleaning routine, or environmental
condition you encounter in ordinary life will compromise its surface.

Frequently Asked Questions: Lifetime Diamond Exchange
Policy
Q: What is a lifetime diamond exchange policy?
A lifetime diamond exchange policy is a guarantee from a jewellery brand that allows a
customer to return a diamond purchased from them at any point in the future and exchange
it for another diamond or piece of jewellery at a defined credit value. Unlike standard return
policies, a lifetime exchange policy does not expire, it remains valid for as long as the buyer
owns the original piece.
Q: Does Solitario offer a lifetime diamond exchange policy?
Yes. Solitario offers a genuine lifetime diamond exchange policy covering all diamonds in its
collection, including lab-grown diamonds. The policy allows customers to exchange their
original diamond toward any piece in Solitario's current collection at the full original invoice
value, with no expiry date and no penalty for normal wear.
Q: Can I exchange a lab-grown diamond under Solitario's policy?
Yes, absolutely. Solitario's lifetime exchange policy applies equally to all lab-grown
diamonds in its collection. Lab-grown diamonds are covered under the same terms as any
other diamond, with no distinction in policy, credit value, or exchange flexibility. This is a
deliberate and important aspect of Solitario's approach: every diamond customer receives
the same level of commitment.
Q: What is the exchange value I receive under Solitario's policy?
Solitario's exchange policy honours the original invoice value of your diamond as exchange
credit. This means the full amount you paid for your diamond can be applied toward a new
piece. If the new piece is of greater value, you pay only the difference. Full terms are
provided in writing with every purchase, and exchange credit is confirmed in writing before
any exchange is initiated.
Q: What is the difference between a diamond exchange policy and a return policy?
A return policy allows you to return a product for a refund within a defined window, typically
7 to 30 days. A diamond exchange policy is different: it does not provide a cash refund but
instead offers exchange credit toward a new purchase. A lifetime exchange policy extends
this exchange entitlement indefinitely, there is no time limit on when you can use it.
Q: Can I upgrade my diamond under Solitario's exchange programme?
Yes. Solitario's exchange programme includes a structured upgrade pathway. If you wish to
exchange your current diamond for one of higher carat weight, better clarity, or a larger
setting, your original invoice value is applied as credit and you pay the difference. Upgrade
exchanges are available across the full Solitario collection.
Q: Do I need the original certificate to use Solitario's exchange policy?
Having the original gemological certificate (IGI or GIA) is helpful and recommended.
However, Solitario's exchange team is able to assist with certificate replacement and
supporting documentation in cases where the original has been lost. We do not use
certificate loss as a reason to void an otherwise valid exchange. Contact our exchange team
directly to discuss your specific situation.
Q: Why does a lifetime exchange policy matter for lab-grown diamond buyers?
Lab-grown diamond buyers sometimes have concerns about the developing secondary
resale market for lab-grown stones. A lifetime exchange policy directly addresses this: it
provides a guaranteed value pathway for your diamond that is independent of external
resale markets. Rather than selling your diamond on the secondhand market, you can
exchange it through Solitario at the original invoice value, giving your investment a defined,
protected future.
